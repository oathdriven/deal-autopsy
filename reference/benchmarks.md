# Benchmarks: what alive sounds like

A coroner needs to know healthy tissue to recognize damage. This file holds what a
PASSED gate sounds like in a real transcript, so FAKED (went through the motions, gate
never cleared) is distinguishable from PASSED.

The lines below are from a real 10-minute call: the builder of this tool selling
against Dr. David Chen, an AI buyer persona tuned to be a jaded, burned, hard-mode
chiropractor who hangs up on bad selling. The call CLOSED ($4K setup + $2K/mo), so it
is not an autopsy subject. It is the control group.

## Passed gates, from the tape

**stage-3, the commitment gate clearing (2:54):**
> Seller: "Before I get into all the weeds, would you be opposed to moving forward
> with me and do a business if everything I'm about to tell you makes sense?"
> Chen (3:12): "No. I wouldn't be opposed if it makes sense. I mean, that's why I took
> the call. Right?"

A clean no, meaning not opposed. Compare the FAKED version: "Ummm... I mean, sure,
let's see what you've got." That is a hedge wearing a yes costume; the gate did not
clear and the spade it leaves in the pocket is made of paper.

**stage-4, run on the prospect's own numbers (3:18 to 4:39):**
> Chen: patient value "800 maybe 1000" · missed calls "about a 3rd... 7, 8 of them" ·
> "maybe half" connect · "3 out of 4" close.
> Seller: "You're looking about 2400 dollars, give or take, in missed calls almost daily."

Agree, agree, agree. The prospect built the case himself. Nobody argues with their own
math.

**stage-5, the test close surfacing the truth where it costs nothing (5:19, 6:03):**
> Seller: "Does that math kinda add up? And am I making sense so far?"
> Chen: "Yeah. I mean, the math makes sense. Hold on... what happens if the leads are
> junk like last time?"

The real objection arrived BEFORE the price. That is the stage doing its job.

**stage-6, the close and the silence (6:50):**
> Seller: "A simple 4000 dollar setup... then it's 2000 dollars a month... What would
> you like to do next?"
> Chen (7:17): "Pause. That's real money." [silence]

## The skip that proved the machine, inside a won call

Even the winning tape demonstrates the causality this tool rules on. The
decision-maker check (part of the stage-2 gate) was skipped in diagnosis. Right on
schedule, at 7:36, the shield appeared:

> Chen: "I mean, look, I'd have to run it by my partner."

Because commitment WAS banked at 2:54, the seller had a spade and used it:

> Seller: "Do your business partners know that you have this problem?"
> Chen (7:47): "Well, I mean, technically, it's my practice. My partners trust me on ops."
> Seller: "Why would they say no?"
> Chen (8:02): "Look, honestly, I got burned by that agency, and I'm scared of doing
> it again."

One skipped check produced the exact predicted symptom 4 minutes later, and the lie
collapsed in 2 questions only because an earlier gate was banked. Skips create
symptoms downstream; banked gates pay for them. That is the whole diagnostic model,
visible in 30 seconds of tape.

## A survived out-of-order run

The same call broke the canonical order at the open. Chen crushed the rapport runway
at 0:07 ("I'm good. Look, I'm between patients, so let's get to it") and the doctor
frame never landed. The call survived because every DEPENDENT stage was still insured
before it ran: pain was diagnosed before anything was presented, commitment existed
(2:54) before the roadmap and price, the stack was flushed before the number. This
tape is the control group for rule 4 in rules.md: sequence is the default path,
clearance is the law. The coroner never rules a death merely because stages came out
of order.

## The cause vs symptom table

The fast test, applied before every ruling:

| Question | Cause | Symptom |
|---|---|---|
| Does it disappear when something ELSE is fixed? | No | Yes |
| Can it be located at one moment in the transcript? | Yes, the skip or fake | Usually smeared across the back half |
| Could the seller have seen it at the time? | Yes, a gate they chose to pass by | No, it arrived later as a surprise |
| Would fixing only THIS have changed the outcome? | Yes | No, the upstream break still kills |

"The prospect went cold after the demo" is a symptom. "The demo was given to a
prospect who never committed to anything" is a cause.
